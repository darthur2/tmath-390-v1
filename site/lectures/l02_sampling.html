Sampling Methods

Simple Random Sample

Definition 2.1

A sample selected in such a way that every element of the population has an equal chance of being chosen is called a simple random sample.

Example 2.1

A teacher wants to survey 10 students out of a class of 50 about their study habits. She writes each student’s name on a piece of paper, puts the slips in a hat, and randomly draws 10 names.

Systematic Sample

Definition 2.2

A systematic sample is a sample in which every \(K^{th}\) element in the sampling frame is selected after a suitable random start for the first element.

Example 2.2

A company wants to inspect the quality of items in a production line. They decide to inspect every 10th item after the first item is chosen randomly. If the first randomly selected item is the 4th, they inspect items 4, 14, 24, and so on.

Stratified Sample

Definition 2.3

A sample is obtained by dividing the population into nonoverlapping groups, called strata, based on some factors. A proportional number of elements are then randomly selected from each stratum. This is called a stratified sample.

Example 2.3

A company wants to survey employee satisfaction. To ensure representation across job roles, the employees are divided into strata based on roles: managers, technical staff, administrative staff, and customer service representatives. The survey then selects 10 employees randomly from each of these predefined groups.

Cluster Sample

Definition 2.4

In cluster sampling, the sampling unit contains groups of elements called clusters instead of individual elements of the population. A cluster is an intact group naturally available in the field.

Example 2.4

A city wants to survey households about public transportation. There are 15 neighborhoods (clusters) in the city. Five neighborhoods are randomly selected, and all households within those neighborhoods are surveyed.

Multistage Sample

Definition 2.5

Multistage sampling involves collecting information from the whole sample, followed by additional data collection either at the same time or later from subsamples.

Example 2.5

A national survey aims to assess high school students’ opinions on standardized testing. First, researchers randomly select 10 states from across the country. Within each selected state, they randomly choose 5 school districts. Next, they randomly select 3 high schools within each of those districts. Finally, within each selected high school, 30 students are randomly surveyed.

Ex: Equal Chance Selection

Example 2.6

In which type of sampling method does every individual of the population have an equal chance of being selected?

This refers to simple random sampling. Every individual in a simple random sample has an equal chance of being selected.

Ex: Selection from Hierarchies

Example 2.6

Which type of sampling method is often used to sample from populations that have natural hierarchies?

Sampling from populations with natural hierarchies often involves multistage sampling. At each step of the process, you sample from one level of the hierarchy.

Ex: Group Selection

Example 2.7

Which type of sampling method randomly selects entire groups of individuals to be part of the sample rather than selecting the individuals directly?

This refers to cluster sampling. In cluster sampling, entire groups, or clusters, are randomly selected and every individual in the group/cluster is included in the sample.

Ex: Subgroup Selection

Example 2.8

Which type of sampling method divides the population into distinct subgroups and randomly selects a fixed number of individuals from each subgroup?

This refers to stratified sampling. Researchers create or define subgroups of the population. They then select a certain number of individuals within each subgroup.
