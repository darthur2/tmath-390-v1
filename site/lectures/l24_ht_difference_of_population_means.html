Hypothesis Test for Difference of Means (Two-Tailed, Equal Variance)

Theorem 27.1

Let \(X_{11}, X_{12}, \dots, X_{1n_1}\) be independent, normally distributed random variables with mean \(\mu_1\) and unknown standard deviation \(\sigma\) and let \(X_{21}, X_{22}, \dots, X_{2n_2}\) be independent, normally distributed random variables with mean \(\mu_2\) and unknown standard deviation \(\sigma\). Consider the test \(H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2\) vs \(H_A: \mu_1 \neq \mu_2\). If we want the Type-I error rate to be no larger than \(\alpha\). Then we would reject \(H_0\) when the following was true:

\[ \left|\frac{\bar{x}_1 - \bar{x}_2}{s_p\sqrt{\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}}} \right| > t_{n_1+n_2-2, 1-\alpha/2} \]

where

\[ s_p = \sqrt{\frac{(n_1-1)s_1^2 + (n_2-1)s_2^2}{n_1 + n_2 - 2}} \]

If the data are non-normal, but \(n_1, n_2\) are sufficiently large, then this is an approximate \(100 \times (1-\alpha)\%\) interval.

A rule of thumb for determining equal variance is that \(\max(s_1^2, s_2^2) \leq 4 \times \min(s_1^2, s_2^2)\)
