Confidence Interval Practice

Ex: Coffee

Question 1

A coffee company wants to estimate the average caffeine content in their large brewed coffee. A random sample of 60 cups is tested, and the average caffeine content is found to be 140 mg per cup. Previous studies have shown that the population standard deviation is 12 mg. Construct a 95% confidence interval for the true mean caffeine content.

n <- 60
sigma <- 12
alpha <- 0.05
xbar <- 140
z_quant <- qnorm(1-alpha/2)

xbar + c(-1, 1)*z_quant*sigma/sqrt(n)
[1] 136.9636 143.0364

Ex: Reading

Question 4

A literacy program wants to estimate the average reading speed (in words per minute) of high school students. A random sample of 40 students had a mean reading speed of 220 words per minute with a standard deviation of 25 words per minute. Construct a 99% confidence interval for the population mean reading speed.

xbar <- 220
n <- 40
s <- 25
alpha <- 0.01
t_quant <- qt(1-alpha/2, n-1)

xbar + c(-1, 1)*t_quant*s/sqrt(n)
[1] 209.296 230.704

Ex: Keyboards

Question 6

A study compares reaction times (in milliseconds) for typing on two different types of keyboards (Mechanical and Membrane). A random sample of 35 users for Mechanical keyboards and 30 users for Membrane keyboards is taken. The results are: \(\bar{x}_{\text{Mech}} = 120, \bar{x}_{\text{Mem}} = 130, s^2_{\text{Mech}} = 18, s^2_{\text{Mem}} = 22\). Construct a 95% confidence interval for the difference between average reaction times (Mechanical minus Membrane).

n_mech <- 35
n_mem <- 30
xbar_mech <- 120
xbar_mem <- 130
s2_mech <- 18
s2_mem <- 22
alpha <- 0.05

max(s2_mech, s2_mem) <= 4*min(s2_mech, s2_mem)
[1] TRUE
t_quant <- qt(1-alpha/2, n_mech + n_mem - 2)
sp <- sqrt(((n_mech-1)*s2_mech + (n_mem-1)*s2_mem)/(n_mech + n_mem - 2))
se <- sp*sqrt(1/n_mech + 1/n_mem)

(xbar_mech - xbar_mem) + c(-1, 1)*t_quant*se
[1] -12.214707  -7.785293

Ex: Teaching

Question 8

A study compares two teaching methods (Traditional and Interactive) based on students’ final exam scores. Two independent groups of students are randomly assigned: 42 to Traditional and 39 to Interactive. The results show that for Traditional, \(\bar{x}_T = 75, s^2_T = 20\), and for Interactive, \(\bar{x}_I = 80, s^2_I = 90\). Construct a 99% confidence interval for the difference in the average final exam scores for the two methods (Interactive minus Traditional).

xbar_T <- 75
xbar_I <- 80
s2_T <- 20
s2_I <- 90
n_T <- 42
n_I <- 39

max(s2_T, s2_I) <= 4*min(s2_T, s2_I)
[1] FALSE
df <- ((s2_T/n_T + s2_I/n_I)^2)/(s2_T^2/(n_T^2*(n_T-1)) + s2_I^2/(n_I^2*(n_I-1)))
alpha <- 0.01
t_quant <- qt(1-alpha/2, df)
se <- sqrt(s2_T/n_T + s2_I/n_I)
(xbar_I - xbar_T) + c(-1, 1)*t_quant*se
[1] 0.5426975 9.4573025
