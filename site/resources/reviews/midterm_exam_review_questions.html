Midterm Exam Review Questions

Here are 48 test questions, two for each of the 24 concepts you listed. Each question is written without explicitly telling students what method to use to solve the problem:

1. Identify Population, Sample, and Sampling Method

Q1. A researcher is studying the effect of a new diet on weight loss. She recruits 50 volunteers from her gym and tracks their weight for 3 months.
Identify the population, the sample, and the sampling method used.

Q2. A school administrator selects 100 student records at random from all high school students in the district to evaluate graduation rates.
Identify the population, the sample, and the sampling method used.

2. Identify Type of Variable

Q3. A survey asks students how many hours they study per week.
Is this variable discrete quantitative, continuous quantitative, or qualitative?

Q4. A study records the blood type (A, B, AB, or O) of each participant.
Is this variable discrete quantitative, continuous quantitative, or qualitative?

3. Choose the Best Visualization Tool

Q5. A teacher wants to show how final exam scores are distributed among 100 students.
What is the best type of plot to use?

Q6. A company collected satisfaction ratings (poor, fair, good, excellent) from 30 customers.
What is the best type of plot to use?

4. Permutation vs. Combination

Q7. In how many ways can 3 students be selected to present from a group of 10 if the order of presentation matters?

Q8. A team of 5 will be selected from 9 applicants. How many different teams are possible?

5. Multiple Combinations

Q9. A club has 8 women and 6 men. In how many ways can a committee be formed with 2 women and 3 men?

Q10. A bakery offers 5 types of muffins and 6 types of scones. A breakfast box includes 2 muffins and 2 scones. How many different breakfast boxes are possible?

6. Compute Probability from Enumerated Sample Space

Q11. Two fair dice are rolled. What is the probability that the sum is a multiple of 4?

Q12. A card is drawn at random from a standard 52-card deck. What is the probability it is a heart or a face card?

7. Compute Conditional or Joint Probability

Q13. The probability a student passes math is 0.8, and the probability a student passes both math and science is 0.5. What is the probability the student passes science given they passed math?

Q14. The probability a person is left-handed is 0.1, and the probability they are left-handed given they are an artist is 0.25. What is the probability someone is a left-handed artist if 5% of the population are artists?

8. Use Inclusion-Exclusion Principle

Q15. In a survey of 200 people, 120 like tea, 80 like coffee, and 30 like both. How many like at least one of the two?

Q16. In a class of 100 students, 65 are in the math club, 40 are in the science club, and 25 are in both. How many are in neither club?

9. Use Complement Rule

Q17. What is the probability that at least one of three fair coins lands heads?

Q18. A password consists of 4 digits, each from 0 to 9. What is the probability that at least two digits are the same?

10. Use Bayes' Theorem

Q19. A disease affects 1% of the population. A test for the disease gives a positive result 95% of the time when the person has it and 2% of the time when the person does not. What is the probability someone has the disease given a positive result?

Q20. 70% of emails are spam. A filter correctly flags 90% of spam and incorrectly flags 5% of non-spam. What is the probability an email is spam given it was flagged?

11. E[X], Var[X], E[aX + b], Var[aX + b]

Q21. A discrete random variable X has values 1, 2, 3 with probabilities 0.2, 0.5, and 0.3 respectively. Find E[X], Var[X], E[2X + 3], and Var[2X + 3].

Q22. A continuous random variable X has PDF f(x)=2xf(x) = 2x on 0<x<10 < x < 1. Find E[X], Var[X], E[4X - 1], and Var[4X - 1].

12. Find and Use CDF

Q23. A discrete random variable X has PMF P(X=1)=0.3,P(X=2)=0.5,P(X=3)=0.2P(X=1)=0.3, P(X=2)=0.5, P(X=3)=0.2. Find the CDF and use it to find P(X≤2)P(X \leq 2).

Q24. A continuous random variable X has PDF f(x)=3x2f(x) = 3x^2 for 0≤x≤10 \leq x \leq 1. Find the CDF of X and use it to compute P(0.2≤X≤0.5)P(0.2 \leq X \leq 0.5).

13. Compute Probabilities from a CDF

Q25. A random variable X has CDF F(x)=1−e−2xF(x) = 1 - e^{-2x} for x≥0x \geq 0. What is P(1≤X≤2)P(1 \leq X \leq 2)?

Q26. A discrete random variable X has CDF F(x)=0F(x) = 0 for x<0x < 0, 0.40.4 for 0≤x<10 \leq x < 1, 0.70.7 for 1≤x<21 \leq x < 2, and 1 for x≥2x \geq 2. What is P(X=1)P(X = 1)?

14. Transformations of CDF

Q27. A continuous random variable X has CDF F(x)=x2F(x) = x^2 for 0≤x≤10 \leq x \leq 1. Let Y = √X. Find the CDF of Y.

Q28. A discrete random variable X has CDF F(x)=0.2F(x) = 0.2 at x = 1, 0.5 at x = 2, 0.9 at x = 3, and 1 at x = 4. Let Y = 5 - X. Find the CDF of Y.

15. Transformations of PMF/PDF

Q29. A random variable X has PDF f(x)=2xf(x) = 2x on [0, 1]. Let Y=X2Y = X^2. Find the PDF of Y.

Q30. A discrete random variable X has PMF P(X=1)=0.3,P(X=2)=0.4,P(X=3)=0.3P(X=1)=0.3, P(X=2)=0.4, P(X=3)=0.3. Let Y = 2X + 1. Find the PMF of Y.

16. Marginal and Independence

Q31. A joint PMF is given:
P(X=1,Y=1)=0.2P(X=1, Y=1)=0.2, P(X=1,Y=2)=0.3P(X=1, Y=2)=0.3, P(X=2,Y=1)=0.1P(X=2, Y=1)=0.1, P(X=2,Y=2)=0.4P(X=2, Y=2)=0.4.
Find the marginal PMF of X and determine if X and Y are independent.

Q32. A joint PDF is f(x,y)=6xyf(x,y) = 6xy for 0≤x≤10 \leq x \leq 1, 0≤y≤10 \leq y \leq 1.
Find the marginal PDF of X and determine whether X and Y are independent.

17. Covariance and Interpretation

Q33. A joint PMF has E[X]=2E[X] = 2, E[Y]=3E[Y] = 3, and E[XY]=7.5E[XY] = 7.5. Find Cov(X, Y) and interpret its meaning.

Q34. A joint PDF has E[X]=1E[X] = 1, E[Y]=2E[Y] = 2, and E[XY]=2.5E[XY] = 2.5. Find Cov(X, Y) and interpret what the sign of the covariance indicates.

18–23. Distribution Identification, Parameters, Probabilities

Q35. A machine produces light bulbs. Each bulb has a 5% chance of being defective. The number of defective bulbs in a batch of 100 is recorded. What type of distribution describes this?

Q36. A random variable represents the number of times a coin is flipped until the first head appears. What distribution does this follow?

Q37. The number of calls arriving at a call center in an hour follows a Poisson distribution with rate 4. What is its mean and variance?

Q38. A random variable follows a Beta distribution with parameters α=2\alpha = 2, β=3\beta = 3. What are the mean and variance?

Q39. Let X be a Binomial random variable with parameters n=10n = 10, p=0.2p = 0.2. What is P(X=2)P(X = 2)?

Q40. Let X follow an Exponential distribution with rate λ=0.5\lambda = 0.5. What is P(X>3)P(X > 3)?

24. Compare Using Z-Scores

Q41. Two students took different tests. Alice scored 85 on a test with mean 80 and standard deviation 5. Bob scored 78 on a test with mean 70 and standard deviation 4. Who performed better relative to their class?

Q42. A marathon runner finishes in 210 minutes in a race where the mean time is 250 minutes with a standard deviation of 30. A cyclist finishes in 140 minutes in a race with a mean of 180 and SD of 25. Who performed better?

25. Normal Transformation and Probabilities

Q43. Let X ~ N(0, 1), and Y = 3X + 2. What are the mean and standard deviation of Y? What is P(Y>5)P(Y > 5)?

Q44. Let Z be standard normal. Define W = -2Z + 1. Find the mean and SD of W, and find P(W<−1)P(W < -1).

26. Find a Normal Quantile

Q45. A normal random variable has mean 50 and standard deviation 10. What value corresponds to the 90th percentile?

Q46. SAT scores are normally distributed with a mean of 1000 and SD of 200. What score is needed to be in the top 5%?
