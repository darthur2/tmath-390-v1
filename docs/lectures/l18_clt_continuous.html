Central Limit Theorem

Sampling Distributions for Non-Normal RVs

We learned that if a collection of independent random variables \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\) are normally distributed, then the sample mean is also normally distributed.

What if instead of being normally distributed, we have one of the following scenarios:

  • \(X_i \overset{iid}{\sim} \text{Unif}(a, b)\)
  • \(X_i \overset{iid}{\sim} \text{Exp}(\lambda)\)
  • \(X_i \overset{iid}{\sim} \text{Gamma}(\alpha, \lambda)\)
  • \(X_i \overset{iid}{\sim} \text{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)\)

Here \(\overset{iid}{\sim}\) is a shortcut for saying the random variables are independent and identically distributed where identically distributed means they each have the same distribution with the same parameters.

Central Limit Theorem

Theorem 18.1

If \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\) are independent and identically distributed random variables with mean \(\text{E}[X_i] = \mu\) and variance \(\text{Var}[X_i] = \sigma^2 < \infty\), then as \(n \rightarrow \infty\):

\[ \sqrt{n}(\bar{X} - \mu) \overset{D}{\rightarrow} N(0, \sigma) \]

For sufficiently large \(n\) this means that:

\[ \bar{X} \overset{\cdot}{\sim} N\left(\mu, \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} \right) \]

Here \(\overset{D}{\rightarrow}\) means converges in distribution and \(\overset{\cdot}{\sim}\) means approximately distributed.

Ex: Machine Life

Example 18.1

The amount of time (in hours) a machine can run before requiring maintenance follows a gamma distribution with shape parameter \(\alpha = 10\) and rate parameter \(\lambda = 2\). A company monitors the times of 50 different machines.

What is the approximate sampling distribution of \(\bar{X}\) for the average lifetime of these 50 machines?

According to the Central Limit Theorem we have the following:

\[ \begin{align} \bar{X} & \overset{\cdot}{\sim} N\left(\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}, \sqrt{\frac{\alpha}{n\lambda^2}}\right) \\ & \overset{\cdot}{\sim} N\left(5, \sqrt{0.05}\right) \end{align} \]

Computing Exact Probabilities for Gamma Means

Theorem 18.2

Let \(X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n\) be independent gamma random variables with shape \(\alpha\) and rate \(\lambda\). Then the following is true:

\[ P(\bar{X} \leq \bar{x}) = F_X(n\times\bar{x}; n \times \alpha, \lambda) \]

where \(F_X(n\times\bar{x}; n \times \alpha, \lambda)\) is the CDF for a gamma distribution with shape parameter \(n \times \alpha\) and rate parameter \(\lambda\).

\[ \begin{align} P(\bar{X} \leq \bar{x}) &= P\left(\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \leq \bar{x}\right) \\ &= P\left(\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \leq n\times\bar{x}\right) \\ &= F_X(n \times \bar{x}; n \times \alpha, \lambda) & \text{Since} \sum_{i=1}^n X_i \sim \text{Gamma}(n\times \alpha, \lambda) \end{align} \]

What is the probability that the average lifetime exceeds 5.5 hours?

According to the Central Limit Theorem we have the following:

\[ \bar{X} \overset{\cdot}{\sim} N\left(5, \sqrt{0.05}\right) \]

mu_xbar <- 5
sigma_xbar <- sqrt(0.05)

1-pnorm(5.5, mu_xbar, sigma_xbar)
[1] 0.01267366
1-pgamma(50*5.5, 50*10, 2)
[1] 0.01461441

mu_xbar <- 5.2
sigma_xbar <- 0.8/sqrt(40)
