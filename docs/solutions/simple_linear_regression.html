Simple Linear Regression

Part 1 - Predicting Sales from Marketing Spend

A company wants to predict weekly sales (in $1,000s) \(y\) based on the amount they spend on marketing (in $100s) \(x\). The data for 30 weeks is provided below:

x <- c(10, 12, 14, 13, 11, 15, 16, 18, 17, 19,
       21, 20, 22, 23, 24, 25, 26, 28, 27, 29,
       30, 32, 31, 33, 35, 34, 36, 38, 37, 39)

y <- c(42, 43, 51, 43, 50, 60, 65, 57, 58, 62, 
       61, 59, 72, 68, 64, 69, 71, 86, 71, 83, 
       90, 87, 90, 83, 101, 83, 88, 96, 92, 103)

Fit a simple linear regression model and then do the following:

Question 1 (4 points)

Report the estimated intercept and slope. Interpret both in context.

mod <- lm(y ~ x)

Question 4 (2 points)

Construct a 95% confidence interval for the slope coefficient.

confint_out <- confint(mod, level = 0.95)

cat("We are 95% confident that the true expected increase in sales for every additional $100 spent on marketing is between", confint_out[2,1]*1000, "and", confint_out[2,2]*1000)
We are 95% confident that the true expected increase in sales for every additional $100 spent on marketing is between 1695.063 and 2147.428

Question 5 (2 points)

Construct a 95% confidence interval for the mean sales when marketing spend is 20. Interpret the interval.

newdata <- data.frame(x = 20)
conf_out <- predict(mod, newdata = newdata, interval = "confidence", level = 0.95)

cat("We are 95% confident that the expected sales for all marketing campaigns with a spend of $2000 is between", conf_out[2]*1000, "and", conf_out[3]*1000)
We are 95% confident that the expected sales for all marketing campaigns with a spend of $2000 is between 60747.91 and 65160.88

Question 6 (2 points)

Construct a 95% prediction interval for a new weekly sales value when marketing spend is 20. Interpret the interval.

newdata <- data.frame(x = 20)
pred_out <- predict(mod, newdata = newdata, interval = "prediction", level = 0.95)

cat("We are 95% confident that the sales for a new marketing campaign with a spend of $2000 is between", pred_out[2]*1000, "and", pred_out[3]*1000)
We are 95% confident that the sales for a new marketing campaign with a spend of $2000 is between 52006.92 and 73901.87

Part 2 - Predicting Viscosity from Ingredient Amount

A chemist is analyzing how the viscosity (in centipoise) \(y\) of a solution depends on the amount of a key ingredient (in grams) \(x\). The following data was collected:

x <- c(1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5, 4.0, 4.5, 5.0, 5.5, 6.0,
       6.5, 7.0, 7.5, 8.0, 8.5, 9.0, 9.5, 10.0, 10.5, 11.0,
       11.5, 12.0, 12.5, 13.0, 13.5)

y <- c(36, 6.5, 37.5, 34, 13, 49, 44.1, 49.2, 26.5, 56, 49.2, 
       57.4, 41.6, 57.6, 42.5, 67, 67, 81.2, 65.1, 53.7, 65.3, 
       74.1, 77.3, 77.2, 75.7)
